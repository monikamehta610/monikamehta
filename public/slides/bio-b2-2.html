B2.2.1

Organelles as Discrete Subunits

Key UnderstandingOrganelles are discrete, specialized subunits within a cell that are adapted for particular functions.

Cells are incredibly complex, resembling microscopic cities. To function efficiently, they rely on compartmentalization—separating different processes into specialized membrane-bound structures called organelles.

Isolate incompatible biochemical processes (e.g., destructive enzymes in lysosomes)
↓
Maintain specific conditions (e.g., lower pH) for optimal enzyme function
↓
Concentrate metabolites and enzymes to increase the rate of reactions
Note: Not all cellular structures are organelles. The cell wall, cytoskeleton, and the cytoplasm itself are generally not classified as organelles, but rather as cellular components.
B2.2.2

Nucleus and Cytoplasm Separation

Key UnderstandingSeparation of the nucleus and cytoplasm allows post-transcriptional modification of mRNA in eukaryotes.

Unlike prokaryotes where transcription and translation happen simultaneously, eukaryotes have a nuclear envelope separating the DNA from the cytoplasm.

Skill: Analyzing Spatial Control By separating transcription (in the nucleus) from translation (at the ribosomes in the cytoplasm), eukaryotic cells can regulate and modify mRNA (e.g., splicing out introns) before proteins are synthesized. This spatial control is crucial for complex life.
B2.2.3

Advantages of Compartmentalization

Key UnderstandingAdvantages of compartmentalization in the cytoplasm, focusing on lysosomes and phagocytic vacuoles.

Compartmentalization provides safe spaces for dangerous cellular operations.

Lysosomes

Contain hydrolytic enzymes for intracellular digestion. The acidic environment inside (pH ~4.5-5.0) is optimal for these enzymes, protecting the rest of the cell from autodigestion.

Phagocytic Vacuoles

Formed when cells engulf large particles or pathogens (e.g., by white blood cells). They fuse with lysosomes to safely digest the contents.

B2.2.4 HL Only

Mitochondrial Structure and Function

Key Understanding (HL) Structural adaptations of mitochondria for aerobic respiration.

Mitochondria generate ATP through cellular respiration. Their double-membrane structure is highly adapted to this function.

  • Outer Membrane: Contains transport proteins for moving pyruvate into the organelle.
  • Inner Membrane: Folded into cristae to increase surface area for the electron transport chain (ETC) and ATP synthase.
  • Intermembrane Space: Small volume allows rapid accumulation of protons ($H^+$) to create a high concentration gradient.
  • Matrix: Contains enzymes for the Krebs cycle and mitochondrial DNA.
B2.2.5 HL Only

Chloroplast Structure and Function

Key Understanding (HL) Structural adaptations of chloroplasts for photosynthesis.

Chloroplasts are the site of photosynthesis in plant cells. The overall reaction transforms CO₂ and H₂O into glucose and oxygen.

Application: Photosynthesis Equation $6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light energy} \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2$
  • Thylakoids: Flattened discs with a small internal volume to rapidly build up proton gradients for ATP synthesis.
  • Granum: Stacks of thylakoids increase surface area for photosystems (light absorption).
  • Stroma: Fluid containing enzymes like Rubisco for the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle).
B2.2.6 HL Only

The Nuclear Double Membrane

Key Understanding (HL) The role of the double membrane of the nucleus.

The nuclear envelope consists of two lipid bilayers. It protects the cell's genetic material from the chemical reactions occurring in the cytoplasm and regulates the flow of molecules (like mRNA and proteins) through nuclear pores.

The Endomembrane System

Key Understanding (HL) Roles of ribosomes, Golgi apparatus, and vesicles in the endomembrane system.

The endomembrane system works together to produce, modify, and transport proteins and lipids.

Rough ER: Ribosomes bound to the ER synthesize proteins for secretion or membranes
↓
Transport Vesicles: Bud off the ER to transport proteins to the Golgi
↓
Golgi Apparatus: Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins into new vesicles
↓
Secretory Vesicles: Transport finished products to the plasma membrane (exocytosis)
